# Open Case Digests — proposed manifesto

**20 October 2026 · Founding charter proposal, not a claim of institutional endorsement**

## The purpose

Philippine legal knowledge should be easier to inspect, question, correct and use. A student should be able to follow a digest back to the exact passage it summarizes. A lawyer should be able to see who reviewed a proposition, which version they reviewed, what later decisions may affect it, and what remains uncertain. A contributor's work should remain attributable and useful beyond a single examination or subscription.

Open Case Digests exists to make that possible through a maintained public commons. Case Digestor may build a sustainable business by providing useful services on top of that commons. The public contribution and the commercial service must each justify the trust placed in them.

## What we mean by trust

We distinguish six questions:

1. **Source:** Which judicial issuance and source artifact are we discussing?
2. **Integrity:** Are these the same bytes as the published version?
3. **Attribution:** Which authenticated person or organization made this particular claim?
4. **Review:** What did they examine, under which rubric, and with what disagreement?
5. **Currency:** What later material has been checked, through what date and coverage?
6. **Authority:** What does the competent court actually say?

Software can help answer these questions. A cryptographic receipt cannot answer all six. Reviewer consensus is evidence of review, not a substitute for judicial authority.

## Our commitments

### 1. Primary authority stays visible

Every public digest should lead back to its source. Separate majority reasoning, concurrences, dissents, procedural orders and subsequent resolutions. If issuance identity is ambiguous, publish the uncertainty or hold the record; do not turn a plausible match into an authoritative fact.

### 2. Corrections strengthen the record

A corrected interpretation must remain connected to its earlier version. Reviewers attest to a particular version and scope; their approval does not silently transfer to later edits. Disagreement may remain unresolved. Users should see withdrawals, supersessions and known coverage gaps.

We never equate a lack of detected negative treatment with confirmation that a proposition remains good law.

### 3. Open means usable and portable

An open commons needs a clear content license, documented formats, meaningful exports and an independent verifier. Free access to a website alone is insufficient. Public releases should remain reusable after the original operator stops operating.

Code licensing and content licensing are separate. The existing MIT code license is not permission to republish someone else's digest, student work or commercial headnote. No content license is adopted retroactively by this manifesto.

### 4. Contributors retain dignity and choice

Contribution is an explicit action with a visible publication preview, understandable terms and a choice about public attribution. Private notes, uploads and client materials do not become public through ordinary saving or AI generation.

Students deserve credit, feedback and an alternative to public participation when coursework is involved. We will not make public profiles, permanent identification or unpaid production quotas a hidden price of education.

### 5. Review is labor

Reliable review needs reading time, training, calibration and dispute resolution. We will measure that work and fund it. A short interface interaction may record a review already completed; it cannot replace the review.

Compensation and recognition must reward careful evidence and useful corrections. Payment, subscriptions and contributor popularity must never purchase a favorable legal conclusion.

### 6. Privacy is part of the architecture

Open legal access does not require indiscriminate redistribution of personal information. We will define who is accountable for publication and for correction or removal requests. Identity verification evidence stays in controlled storage with an appropriate retention policy.

Public attribution is optional and specific. Hashes, addresses and identifiers will be assessed for linkability; we will not call them anonymous merely because they omit a name. Public replication can make recall impossible, so suitability for publication must be decided before dissemination.

### 7. Credentials make bounded claims

“Reviewed by two verified practitioners on this date” describes an auditable event. It does not imply approval by the Supreme Court, IBP, a university, or the profession as a whole.

A contribution portfolio records work and its review history. It is not a license to practice, professional accreditation, a promise of employment, or a ranking of human worth.

### 8. Technology serves demonstrated needs

Users will not need cryptocurrency or face gas charges. The operator must still fund hosting, review, security and continuity.

A blockchain may eventually help independent institutions share control of a record. It earns its place only when its trust properties are needed and its operating costs are justified. The value of the commons must survive a change of database, AI provider, storage network or consensus implementation.

### 9. Commercial success pays for service

Case Digestor can charge for time saved, private workspaces, teaching and study workflows, integrations, hosted APIs, support and service commitments. It should earn repeat use through quality and usefulness.

Published open material remains available under its published license. Competitors may reuse it. Our advantage must come from sustained curation, distribution, responsiveness, service quality and relationships, rather than an assertion of ownership over Philippine law.

### 10. Governance can challenge the founder

The editor, operator, reviewer, identity verifier, institutional sponsor and commercial owner have different responsibilities. No single metric or wallet balance should control all of them.

Establish independent editorial participation, conflict disclosures, appeals, transparent policy changes and an emergency removal process before claiming a federated commons. Named institutions are partners only after they agree.

### 11. Public benefit is measured

Measure whether people can reach sources, understand qualifications, find useful corrections and complete real legal-learning or research tasks. Include provincial, low-bandwidth and assistive-technology use in evaluation.

Count reliable, maintained propositions and recurring useful use. Blockchain height, raw digest count and contribution leaderboards do not by themselves measure access to justice.

### 12. Failure must leave something useful

If the company closes or the protocol experiment fails, publish lawful export snapshots, verifier specifications and a clear account of what is and is not maintained. Give users a path to retrieve private work subject to applicable duties.

A smaller, well-maintained commons is an acceptable outcome. A permanent record of unreliable claims dressed as certainty is not.

## A concrete founding promise

> For each public release, make its source, exact version, review scope, rights basis, correction history and known limitations inspectable. Make the verification process reproducible. Preserve private work. Earn the commercial business by making this knowledge more useful.

This promise is the proposed deterministic goal. Commercial viability, complete legal correctness and institutional adoption remain empirical outcomes to test.

## Adoption and accountability

Before public adoption, the founder, a Philippine legal/editorial lead and a privacy/security lead should resolve the content license, reviewer agreement, publication policy, conflict process and appeals ownership. This document supplies an ideology and decision standard; it does not enact those agreements.

Report quarterly: coverage and exclusions; material corrections; reviewer throughput and paid effort; complaint resolution; access and usability; operating cost; and whether the PWA benefits enough to support the work.
